BWhy four strokes take two full turns Plain language + engineering notes

One up-or-down trip of a piston is half a crankshaft turn (180 degrees). Because the cylinder needs four distinct trips to breathe in fresh mixture, squeeze it, burn it and push out exhaust, a complete four-stroke cycle takes two full revolutions (720 degrees).

Only the third stroke (Power) pushes the piston down with burning gas to produce useful torque. The other three strokes borrow momentum stored in the rotating crankshaft and flywheel.

In a four-cylinder inline engine like the RAV4 X's M20A-FKS, the four cylinders are staggered 180 degrees apart in the 720-degree cycle (firing order 1-3-4-2). Whenever one cylinder is pushing on its power stroke, the other three are taking turns handling intake, compression and exhaust.

That staggered firing turns intermittent bursts inside individual cylinders into steady spinning torque at the crankshaft output flange, ready to enter the transmission.

Engineering detail · Four-stroke kinematics, valve timing & pressure-volume work (Heywood)

In a four-stroke spark-ignition engine (Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals), the camshafts rotate at exactly half crankshaft speed (1:2 ratio) so each intake and exhaust valve opens once every 720 crank degrees.

  • Spark advance: Ignition begins shortly before top dead centre (TDC) so peak cylinder pressure builds just after TDC while the piston starts downward.
  • Pumping vs. work loop: On the full Inkworks drafting sheet's pressure-volume (P-V) chart, the area enclosed by the compression and expansion strokes represents positive indicated work, while the lower intake/exhaust loop represents pumping work.
  • Simplifications in this demo: Valve lift curves, manifold pressure scaling and the 10:1 clearance volume in the stock Inkworks demo are smooth closed-form teaching models, not measured M20A-FKS dyno traces.
